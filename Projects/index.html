Independent product + research project

Making everyday visual information accessible.

Freiheit explores how multimodal AI can help people with blindness or low visibility understand the visual information around them — through natural conversation.

Watch the prototype on Youtube ↗ Explore Freiheit ↓ GitHub ↗
The beginning

It started with an observation.

In a crowded Berlin station, I saw a blind person struggling to find an exit.

It made me wonder whether emerging AI could help make some of the visual information that surrounds us more accessible.

So I started building Freiheit.

Everyday visual information

A lot of everyday life is visual.

Freiheit is being developed around the small pieces of visual information that sighted people often access without thinking about them.

Shop

Products, prices & choices

Identify products, distinguish similar packaging, read prices and explore discounts while shopping.

Eat

Food & nutrition

Read ingredients, allergens, nutrition information and dates on food packaging.

Wear

Clothing & style

Understand colours, patterns, clothing types and sizes — and explore combinations based on personal preferences.

Live

Home & appliances

Interpret washing machines, stoves, ovens, displays, controls and everyday household objects.

Read

Labels & information

Read visual text on products, packaging and medicine. Information access is different from medical advice.

Explore

Stations & public spaces

Explore signs, platform information, exits, street names and public transport information. These uses require stronger safety validation.

Everyday questions

Questions sighted people answer without thinking.

For many people, these questions are answered simply by looking. Freiheit explores how that visual information could become accessible through conversation.

“Is this product on discount?”

“What does this washing-machine display??”

Small questions. Everyday decisions. A large amount of visual information.

The hard problem

AI can be wrong.

For an ordinary chatbot, an incorrect answer may be frustrating. In a railway station, near a road or around physical hazards, an incorrect answer can become dangerous.

Freiheit is therefore not only about what multimodal AI can do. It is also about reliability, uncertainty, failure modes and the conditions under which an assistive system should — or should not — be trusted.
